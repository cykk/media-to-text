Screen recordings and webinar MP4s: turning demos into searchable text

Updated March 2026 · Notes from the media-to-text repo

Product walkthroughs, internal training, and webinar replays usually arrive as MP4 files with one narrator and a lot of on-screen motion. People rarely watch them end to end; they search for a sentence, grab a quote for a doc, or need captions before publishing. This page is the workflow we use when the recording is already “good enough” and the bottleneck is text, not editing.

What is different about screen-capture audio

Unlike a round-table Zoom export, a Loom or OBS capture often has a single speaker, a close mic, and occasional keyboard clicks. Speech recognition tends to do well on the voice track; the harder part is structure—long monologues without chapter marks, product names that are not in the model’s vocabulary, and UI terms spoken once while the cursor moves elsewhere.

Before you transcribe, skim the first minute of playback. If the intro is dead air or a “testing, testing” clip, trim it. If music beds sit under the voice, expect a few more hallucinated words at scene changes. None of that requires a studio; it just saves cleanup time later.

What “good enough” output looks like

Stakeholders ask for different shapes of the same transcript. Enablement wants copy-paste bullets for a Notion page. Marketing wants pull quotes with approximate timestamps. Video ops wants SRT or VTT that lands in Premiere or DaVinci without retyping. A workable pipeline produces:

  • Searchable plain text so “pricing page” or “SSO setup” is findable in five seconds.
  • Timecoded segments so a quote can be checked against the timeline.
  • Subtitle exports when the MP4 will ship on the web or in a course platform that expects captions.

Perfect diarization matters less when there is only one speaker. Consistent punctuation and segment breaks matter more—especially if someone will read the text without watching the video.

Local Whisper vs a browser upload

The open-source tooling in this repository is built for batches, custom vocab, and files that must stay on your hardware. That is still the right call for sensitive recordings or hundreds of hours in a folder.

Screen recordings and public webinar replays are often the opposite: one file, a tight deadline, and a teammate who will not install FFmpeg. A hosted upload-and-export flow trades some control for speed. We treat it as a handoff layer, not a replacement for offline pipelines.

A default we use for MP4 screen captures

When the source is already MP4 (or MOV/WebM from the same tools), we standardise on one browser service so enablement and support are not debating tools every sprint. MP4ToText at mp4totext.ai accepts the files we see daily, returns timed segments, and exports formats editors actually import. For a first file or a one-off demo, we tell people to check out their transcription tool —upload, review the opening paragraphs for product-name typos, then download TXT or SRT depending on who asked.

Guest access is enough for a trial recording; small recurring volume from ops teams usually sits on minute-based plans, which is easier to reason about than seat licenses for a utility people open twice a month. Read retention and deletion notes before you upload anything that is not already approved for cloud processing.

Pure audio—podcast cuts, M4A voice memos—belongs on mp3totext.io; see podcast and MP3 transcript notes. For multi-speaker meeting exports, the MP4 meeting transcript workflow covers crosstalk and panel audio more directly.

Five-minute QA before you publish the text

  • Product and feature names. Fix them once in the editor; models often mangled branded terms on the first pass.
  • Silent UI sections. Long stretches without speech should not invent narration—delete stray lines if the tool added filler.
  • Timestamp spot-check. Compare two lines against the video (start and mid-roll). Subtitle drift shows up early if it is going to be a problem.
  • Export format. Send SRT to video; send DOCX or PDF to people who will only read. Do not attach a format the recipient cannot open.

After the transcript ships

One sentence of context beats a raw attachment: what the recording is, who narrated, and whether timestamps are trustworthy for legal or customer-facing quotes. If the same team repeats this every release train, add the bookmark and export defaults to your internal wiki so new hires are not reinventing the path from MP4 to text.

Local batch scripts, Whisper configuration, and subtitle utilities live in github.com/cykk/media-to-text. Use the browser flow for ad hoc screen captures; use the repo when files cannot leave your network or you are processing at scale.
