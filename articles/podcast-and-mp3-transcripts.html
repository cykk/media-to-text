Podcast and MP3 transcripts without standing up a local stack

Updated March 2026 · Notes from the media-to-text repo

Video gets most of the attention in meeting workflows, but half our requests arrive as MP3, M4A, or WAV—podcast cuts, interview audio, and phone voice memos. The output needs are the same: searchable text, optional timestamps, and an export someone can quote from.

Files that show up in audio-only requests

Podcast hosts export MP3 or M4A from their DAW or hosting provider. Journalists receive compressed audio from sources. Ops teams forward WhatsApp or iPhone voice notes converted to M4A. None of these need a video pipeline; uploading them through a video-first tool works but feels clumsy when the UI is built around drag-and-drop MP4.

Normalise loudness if you can (even −16 LUFS as a rough target helps recognition on quiet speech). Split episodes longer than your provider’s per-file cap before upload. Keep the original filename with episode number and guest name—downstream search will thank you.

Browser path for MP3 and podcast audio

We send audio-only work to MP3 to Text (mp3totext.io) when the file is MP3, WAV, M4A, FLAC, or similar—no video track, no subtitle timeline to worry about. The flow matches what non-technical contributors already understand: upload, wait, skim, export to TXT or DOCX, paste quotes into a doc. For show notes, timestamps matter; for a quick memo, plain text is enough.

That site is our counterpart to the MP4 bookmark ( mp4totext.ai for video and mixed libraries). Same family of product decisions—browser-only, minute-based pricing at scale, guest tryouts for a single file—but the defaults assume audio producers rather than Zoom exports.

When to keep Whisper local instead

Batch transcribing an entire podcast back catalogue, fine-tuning vocabulary for names, or keeping raw audio inside a VPC still belongs in this repository’s scripts. The hosted tools are for turnaround: one episode, one interview, one voice note someone needs tomorrow.

Quick QA before you forward the transcript

  • Scan the first minute of text for garbage homophones on brand names.
  • If music beds sit under speech, expect a few dropped words—note that in the handoff.
  • Export format: TXT for Slack, DOCX for editors, SRT only if this audio will be merged back to video.
