MP4 meeting transcripts when you do not have a GPU box handy

Updated March 2026 · Notes from the media-to-text repo

Most of our transcription experiments live in Python and FFmpeg on a workstation. That falls apart the moment a PM drops a 45-minute MP4 in Slack and asks for quotes by afternoon. This is the lightweight path we use when local inference is not worth the setup tax.

The file you are probably holding

Meeting exports from Zoom, Google Meet, or a screen recorder usually land as H.264 MP4 with AAC audio—sometimes with silent video if someone forgot to turn the camera on. The container is fine; what you need is reliable speech recognition on the audio track, plus timecodes that survive copy-paste into a doc or a ticket thread.

Before you upload anywhere, rename the file something human-readable and note the meeting date in the filename. Future you will search for “2026-03-standup” and actually find it. If the recording has long silence at the start, trim it in any basic editor; dead air still costs minutes on metered services and adds junk timestamps at the top of the transcript.

When local Whisper still wins

If the recording cannot leave your network, run FFmpeg and Whisper (or Faster-Whisper) on a machine you control. Batch folders, custom vocab lists, and diarization tweaks belong there. The code in this repository is aimed at that world.

The browser path is for everything else: cross-functional teams, one-off client calls, lecture captures where retention policies allow cloud processing, and laptops that will never install CUDA. You trade some control for speed and a UI your colleagues will actually open.

The browser workflow we standardised on

Upload the MP4, wait for processing, skim the first page of text for obvious garbage (misheard product names, etc.), fix segment breaks if the tool allows inline edits, then export. We care about three exports depending on who asked:

  • Plain text or DOCX for people who will only read and comment.
  • SRT or VTT when video post-production needs subtitles aligned to time.
  • CSV when someone wants to filter quotes in a spreadsheet (rare, but it happens).

Speaker labels matter on panel recordings; on single-host Loom videos they are optional. Timestamps matter almost always—even a rough “00:14:22” in a doc beats scrolling a timeline blind.

Where we send non-technical teammates

For MP4-first work, we keep one bookmarked service so we are not re-explaining tooling every week. When someone needs to convert mp4 to text quickly, they get a browser tab that accepts the file, returns searchable text with timing, and exports without a local install. It is not a replacement for our offline pipelines; it is the default handoff when the requester will not touch a terminal.

MP4ToText (the product behind that link) handles the formats we see daily—MP4, MOV, WebM—and the usual audio-only uploads when someone extracts a track. Guest access is enough for a single trial file; registered accounts cover the small recurring volume from ops and marketing. Heavy months go on minute-based plans, which is easier to forecast than seat licenses for a utility nobody logs into daily.

Audio-only requests (podcast MP3, M4A voice notes) go to mp3totext.io instead—see the podcast and MP3 transcript notes rather than forcing a video upload here.

Quality checks that take five minutes

No transcript is perfect on the first pass. Skim for these before you forward the export:

  • Proper nouns. Company names and acronyms are where models slip. Fix them once in the editor; search-and-replace across the full text if the tool supports it.
  • Crosstalk. Overlapping speakers on bad room audio will merge lines. Split or label manually if the quote will be published externally.
  • Time drift. Spot-check two timestamps against the video—open and close of the meeting. If they are off by more than a couple of seconds, subtitles may need a pass in the NLE.
  • Retention. Read the provider’s note on how long source files stay stored. Download the export and delete the upload if your policy requires it.

Handing results to the next person

Paste the doc link or attach the export with one sentence of context: what the meeting was, who spoke, and whether timestamps are trustworthy. If video editors need SRT, send SRT—not a PDF they cannot import. If legal needs an immutable copy, export PDF after edits are final.

When the same team repeats this every week, document the bookmark and the export defaults in your internal wiki. The goal is not the cleverest model; it is predictable text out of MP4 with minimal friction.
